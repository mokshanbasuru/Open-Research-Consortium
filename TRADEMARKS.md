# Trademark Policy

This policy governs use of the name and marks of the Open Research Consortium (the "Project"). It is modelled on the trademark policies of established open-source projects and foundations. Its purpose is to protect the community and the public from confusion about the origin, endorsement, and quality of work associated with the Project, while keeping the Project's content as free to use as possible.

## Marks

"Open Research Consortium" and any associated logos or visual identity (the "Marks") are claimed as unregistered trademarks of the Project. The Project is not currently incorporated, and the Marks are held by the Founding Maintainer on behalf of the Project, as described in [Section 8 of `GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md#8-assets-and-legal-entity).

## Relationship to the Content Licenses

The licenses that apply to the Project's research, documentation, and software grant broad rights to use, modify, and redistribute that content. They do not grant any right to use the Marks, except as required to comply with those licenses (for example, to reproduce a copyright notice or provide attribution). Use of the Marks is governed by this policy alone.

## Permitted Use

No permission is required to:

- refer to the Project by name in order to describe it accurately, to cite its research, or to state that your work is derived from or compatible with the Project's work (nominative use);
- link to the Project, or quote its name in articles, papers, reviews, and discussion; and
- include required attribution and notices under the applicable license.

When making such references, use the name in plain text, do not alter it, and do not suggest sponsorship or endorsement.

## Restricted Use

Without written permission from the Project, you may not:

- use the Marks as, or as part of, the name of a product, service, organisation, domain name, social media account, or software package;
- use the Marks in a way that suggests the Project has reviewed, endorsed, or published your work when it has not;
- use confusingly similar names or logos; or
- use the Marks in connection with fundraising, membership, or credentialing.

## Forks and Derivative Works

You are free to fork the Project's content under its licenses. A fork or derivative work that is distributed publicly must use a name that is clearly distinct from the Marks, and must state that it is not the official Project. Content that has been modified must not be presented as the Project's published research.

## Requests and Reporting

To request permission for a use not covered above, or to report suspected misuse of the Marks, open an issue labelled `trademark`. Matters involving personal or confidential information may be raised through the process in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) or the private reporting channel described in [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
