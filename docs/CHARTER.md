# Open Research Consortium Charter

This Charter states the purpose, scope, and foundational principles of the Open Research Consortium (the "Project"). It is the highest-ranking governance document of the Project. Where any other document conflicts with this Charter, this Charter prevails. Operating procedures are defined in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../GOVERNANCE.md).

## 1. Mission

The Project exists to produce rigorous, reproducible, and neutral research that anyone may read, reuse, and build upon, and to maintain the open tooling and community processes that make such work possible.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In Scope

- Independent and collaborative research in any domain, published with its methods, sources, and data availability statement.
- Open-source tooling that supports the research workflow, including conversion, validation, and publication utilities.
- Documentation, templates, and processes that improve the quality and accessibility of the above.

### 2.2 Out of Scope

- Advocacy, campaigning, or content whose primary purpose is to promote a political, ideological, or commercial position.
- Confidential, classified, or otherwise restricted material, and personal data of identifiable individuals.
- Operational material designed to facilitate unauthorised access to, or damage of, systems or persons. Security research is welcome and is subject to the dual-use provisions of [`research/README.md`](../research/README.md).

## 3. Foundational Principles

1. **Open licensing.** All content in the Project is released under an open license, as set out in Section 5.
2. **Meritocracy.** Authority within the Project derives from demonstrated, sustained contribution. Organisational affiliation and financial support do not confer governance rights.
3. **Transparency.** Decisions, and the reasoning behind them, are made and recorded in public. Private channels are reserved for security vulnerabilities, Code of Conduct reports, and personnel matters.
4. **Independence.** The Project is vendor-neutral. No single organisation may control the Project, and conflicts of interest are disclosed and managed in accordance with [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../GOVERNANCE.md).
5. **Reproducibility.** Research published by the Project must provide sufficient detail for an independent party to evaluate and, where applicable, reproduce its findings.

## 4. Editorial Neutrality

Neutrality is a property of method, not of subject. The Project may examine contested topics, provided the analysis meets the following standards.

1. **Objectivity.** Claims are supported by cited evidence, transparent methodology, or reproducible code. Limitations and counter-evidence are stated.
2. **Even-handedness.** Competing positions are represented fairly and in their strongest credible form. Advocacy for a political or partisan outcome is not accepted.
3. **Civility.** Ideas and methods are criticised on their merits. Incitement, harassment, and promotion of violence or illegal activity are prohibited.

Submissions that do not meet these standards are returned to the author with reasons. Conduct that violates these standards, or the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), is addressed through the enforcement process defined in that document, which provides for graduated responses and due process. No sanction is applied automatically.

## 5. Licensing and Intellectual Property

1. **Content licenses.** Research, documentation, and governance documents are licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (`CC-BY-4.0`). Software, scripts, automation, and configuration are licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0 (`Apache-2.0`). The authoritative per-path mapping is maintained in `REUSE.toml`.
2. **Inbound equals outbound.** A contribution is licensed under the same license as the path it modifies. Contributors retain copyright in their contributions. The Project requires no copyright assignment and no contributor license agreement.
3. **Developer Certificate of Origin.** Every contribution must be accompanied by a sign-off under the Developer Certificate of Origin, version 1.1, as described in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
4. **Irrevocability.** Rights granted under an open license to material already released are not withdrawn by any later change to this Charter or to the Project's practices.
5. **Marks.** The name "Open Research Consortium" and associated marks are governed by the [Trademark Policy](../TRADEMARKS.md), which is separate from, and does not restrict, the rights granted by the content licenses.

## 6. Governance

The Project is governed by its Maintainers in accordance with [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../GOVERNANCE.md). During the Founding Phase defined there, the Founding Maintainer holds final decision-making authority, exercised in public and subject to the limits stated in that document. The Project transitions to an elected Steering Committee when the criteria in that document are met.

## 7. Amendment

This Charter may be amended only through a Consortium Proposal as described in [`proposals/README.md`](../proposals/README.md), subject to a public comment period of at least fourteen days and approval by at least two-thirds of all active Maintainers. During the Founding Phase, an amendment additionally requires the concurrence of the Founding Maintainer, and where the Founding Maintainer is the sole Maintainer, the Founding Maintainer decides following the comment period and written responses to all substantive objections.

No amendment may withdraw rights already granted under the licenses referred to in Section 5, nor adopt for existing content a license that is not an open license.
